# Canada and the US at a glance

You live in Canada and mostly trade US stocks, so you deal with two systems at once. This table puts them side by side, as of 2026. Each row names the lesson that teaches the topic. Rules, limits and fees change, so follow the source link before you rely on a number.

| Topic | United States | Canada |
|---|---|---|
| Currency | US dollar (USD) | Canadian dollar (CAD). A US stock is priced in USD even when you hold it at a Canadian broker. |
| Main exchanges | NYSE, Nasdaq and Cboe | TSX, TSX Venture, Cboe Canada and the CSE |
| Regular hours | 9:30 a.m. to 4 p.m. ET ([NYSE](https://www.nyse.com/markets/hours-calendars)) | 9:30 a.m. to 4 p.m. ET ([TSX hours and calendar](https://www.tsx.com/en/trading/calendars-and-trading-hours)) |
| Time zone | Eastern Time for NYSE and Nasdaq; Toronto is also on Eastern Time | Eastern Time (Toronto) |
| Market regulators | SEC (the federal regulator) and FINRA (the self-regulatory body for broker-dealers) | Provincial securities commissions such as the OSC in Ontario, and CIRO (the self-regulatory body for investment dealers) |
| If your broker fails | SIPC covers up to US$500,000 per customer capacity, of which US$250,000 can be cash ([SIPC](https://www.sipc.org/for-investors/what-sipc-protects)) | CIPF covers up to C$1 million for general accounts (cash, margin, TFSA, FHSA), with separate C$1 million limits for registered retirement accounts and for RESPs ([CIPF](https://www.cipf.ca/cipf-coverage/about-cipf-coverage)) |
| Central bank | Federal Reserve | Bank of Canada |
| Settlement | T+1 since May 28, 2024 | T+1 since May 27, 2024 ([CIRO](https://www.ciro.ca/news-room/publications/amendments-umir-and-idpc-rules-facilitate-investment-industrys-move-t1-settlement)) |
| Tax on a US dividend paid to you | The treaty limits US withholding to 15% for a Canadian resident, claimed with Form W-8BEN ([treaty text](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-trty/canada.pdf)) | An RRSP's US dividends are exempt from US withholding under Article XXI of the treaty, according to an [IRS letter ruling](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-wd/0810013.pdf) issued to specific requesters |

SIPC and CIPF cover the loss of your cash and securities if a broker fails. Neither covers losses from falling prices.

## The accounts you will use

A Canadian investor typically has some mix of these accounts. All of them can hold US stocks, but the tax treatment differs.

| Account | What it is |
|---|---|
| TFSA | Tax-free savings account. Gains are not taxed. The 2026 annual limit is C$7,000 ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/registered-plans-administrators/pspa/mp-rrsp-dpsp-tfsa-limits-ympe.html)). |
| RRSP | Registered retirement savings plan. Contributions are tax-deductible, and withdrawals are taxed as income. |
| FHSA | First home savings account, for a first home purchase. |
| Cash account | A taxable account where you trade only with money you have. |
| Margin account | A taxable account where you can borrow from the broker to trade. |

Account rules, limits and the choice between them are in [Account types for trading](../02-accounts-currency-and-taxes/01-account-types-for-trading.md). The two brokers the book uses are compared in [Wealthsimple and IBKR compared](../02-accounts-currency-and-taxes/02-wealthsimple-and-ibkr.md).

## US accounts you will hear about

US articles and videos often mention the IRA and the 401(k). These are tax-advantaged retirement accounts for US taxpayers. You will not use them, so read those parts as background. When a US source says "max out your 401(k)", the Canadian counterpart is closer to the RRSP.
