# The 30-minute routine

The book is built for 30 minutes a day. One lesson fits one session, so a steady week finishes five lessons. This page shows how to split the time and fit it around the market.

## The three blocks

| Minutes | Block | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 15 | Read | Read the lesson. Work through each worked example with a calculator. |
| 5 | Try it | Do the exercises, then open the answers and compare. |
| 10 | Practice | Do the practice task at the end of the lesson: a chart drill set, a paper trade, a journal entry or a look-up on a public data site. |

Do the exercises before you open any answer. If you miss one, find the step where your number split from the answer. That step is what to re-read.

## The weekly review

Once a week, replace one lesson with a 30 minute review of your trade journal. Read the week's entries, mark each trade against its plan, and write down one change for next week. The journal lessons in part 4 show how. If you have no trades yet, review your paper trades. If you have neither, redo the week's hardest exercises.

## Fitting it around US market hours

US stocks trade from 9:30 a.m. to 4 p.m. ET. Toronto is in the same time zone, so the clock on your wall matches the market clock, and both change with daylight saving on the same days.

- Reading and exercises can happen at any time, so do them when you are least tired.
- Do the practice block when it fits the task. A chart drill can run any time. A paper trade on an intraday setup needs the market open, so keep that for a session between 9:30 a.m. and 4 p.m. ET.
- The first 30 minutes after the open and the last 30 minutes before the close are the busiest. If you hold a live position, watch it then and leave the lesson for later.
- Swing trading lessons need only closing prices, so you can do them after 4 p.m. ET.

## Weekends and long lessons

Weekends are good for a longer session: two lessons, or one lesson and a review. Markets are closed, so use the time for chart drills, journal reviews and reading-heavy parts such as company financials.

If a lesson takes two days, that is normal. Split it at the Try it box: read on day one, do the exercises and practice on day two. Do not skip the exercises to keep pace. A slower week that you understood beats a faster week you did not.

If you miss a day, pick up where you left off. Do not double up the next day.
