## Cheat sheet

### Timeline of the cases

| Case | Date | What broke | Risk lesson for you |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1929 crash | Oct 1929 | Leverage: buyers put down about 10% | Size so that no fall forces a sale; a loan of 90% is wiped out by a 10% fall |
| 1987 crash | Oct 19, 1987 | A selling rule (portfolio insurance) | Rules that sell into a fall add to it; stops cluster |
| LTCM | 1998 | Leverage (28 to 1) and lender discipline | Leverage turns a small spread move into a large loss of equity |
| Dot-com | 1999 to 2001 | Price needed years of growth; conflicted research | Ask what the price assumes; check who is paid when you buy |
| 2008 crisis | 2007 to 2009 | Leverage (up to 40 to 1), short-term funding, counterparty | Funding and counterparty risk; assets that share one driver |
| Flash crash | May 6, 2010 | Liquidity; a volume-based sell algorithm | Use limit orders in fast markets; expect worse fills |
| COVID crash | March 2020 | Liquidity, even in Treasuries | Safe assets can fall for a few days; circuit breakers pause, not stop |
| GameStop | Jan 2021 | Short interest, borrow cost, clearing margin | A short has no loss limit; brokers can change rules in a day |
| Archegos | Mar 2021 | Swap leverage, concentration, hidden exposure | Exposure divided by capital is the number to know |
| SVB | Mar 2023 | Duration, uninsured deposits, run | Safe bonds still have price risk; check where cash is held |
| Rate shock | 2022 | Duration across all bonds, discount rates | List what in your accounts is rate-sensitive |

### Formulas

| Quantity | Formula | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Equity left after a fall | \((V(1 - f) - L)/(V - L)\) | \(V\) price paid, \(L\) loan, \(f\) fall |
| Fall that wipes equity | \(1/	ext{leverage}\) | 28 to 1 is 3.6%; 40 to 1 is 2.5% |
| Leverage | assets / equity | Exposure / capital for swaps |

### Red flags for scams and promotions

- A private group about one stock, or pressure to act today.
- A claim of inside information or a special link to the company.
- A small, unfamiliar stock with little volume.
- Instructions to buy at a set price, quantity and date.
- No disclosure of payment or positions; fake credentials.
- A win rate with no sample size, payoff or audit.

### Checks before you pay or act

1. Search the person and firm in the CSA National Registration Search; note the date.
2. For a CIRO-regulated advisor, read the CIRO AdvisorReport.
3. Check the OSC and CSA investor alerts lists.
4. Confirm your own broker is a CIRO and CIPF member, and what CIPF covers (not market losses).
5. If in doubt, report to your provincial regulator and the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre.

### Rules in this part

1. Work out the fall that wipes you out before using leverage.
2. Count exposure, not account balance.
3. Know the duration of what you hold.
4. Expect exits to fill worse in a fast market.
5. Know where your cash sits and who protects it.
6. Ask who profits if you buy.
