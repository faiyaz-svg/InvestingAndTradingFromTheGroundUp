# Economic calendar

The US and Canadian releases from [The economic calendar](../11-macro-and-economic-data/07-the-economic-calendar.md), with what each one measures and what tends to move on a surprise. Times are Eastern Time (Toronto's time zone) and follow daylight saving. Each time comes from the publisher's own page, as of 2026. The agencies' schedules are the source of truth, and calendar sites copy them, so check the agency the day before.

## US releases

| Release | Agency | Usual day | Time (ET) | What it measures | What tends to move |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CPI and core CPI | BLS | Set by the [BLS calendar](https://www.bls.gov/schedule/news_release/) (October 2026 CPI was scheduled for November 10, 2026) | 8:30 a.m. | Prices paid by urban households; core leaves out food and energy | Rate expectations, Treasury yields, the dollar and stock futures |
| PPI | BLS | Set by the BLS calendar | 8:30 a.m. | What domestic producers receive for their output | Same as CPI |
| Employment Situation (jobs report) | BLS | Usually a Friday | 8:30 a.m. | Payrolls, unemployment rate and average hourly earnings | Rate expectations and yields first; stocks react when the details settle |
| JOLTS | BLS | Set by the BLS calendar | 10:00 a.m. | Job openings, hires and separations | Rate expectations (labour demand) |
| Weekly jobless claims | Labor Department | Thursdays (a Thursday holiday moves it) | 8:30 a.m. | New claims for unemployment insurance | Small effect from one week; compare with a four-week average |
| GDP | BEA | Set by the [BEA schedule](https://www.bea.gov/news/schedule) (advance estimate about a month after the quarter ends) | 8:30 a.m. | Output of the economy, at an annual rate, revised twice | Rates up on strength; stocks mixed |
| Personal income and outlays (PCE) | BEA | Set by the BEA schedule (October 2026 data was scheduled for November 25, 2026) | 8:30 a.m. | The PCE price index, the Fed's preferred inflation measure, and spending | Same as CPI, though CPI comes out first |
| Advance retail sales | Census Bureau | Set by the [Census calendar](https://www.census.gov/economic-indicators/calendar-listview.html) | 8:30 a.m. | Spending at stores, restaurants and online sellers, in dollars of the month | Rates up on strength; stocks mixed |
| ISM Manufacturing PMI | ISM | First business day of the month | 10:00 a.m. | A survey of purchasing managers; 50 is the line between growth and decline | Rates up on strength; stocks mixed |
| ISM Services PMI | ISM | Third business day of the month | 10:00 a.m. | The same survey for services | Same |
| FOMC statement | Federal Reserve | Last day of each meeting | 2:00 p.m. | The rate decision and the Fed's view of the economy | Everything: rates, the dollar, stocks |
| Fed balance sheet (H.4.1) | Federal Reserve | Thursdays | 4:30 p.m. | The Fed's assets and liabilities | Weekly changes are noisy and have no proven link to short-term stock moves ([QE, QT and liquidity](../11-macro-and-economic-data/10-qe-qt-and-liquidity.md)) |

Direction of a reaction, from [How markets react to data](../11-macro-and-economic-data/08-how-markets-react-to-data.md): a hotter inflation print tends to lift rates and the dollar and lower stocks, and a cooler one does the reverse. Strong jobs data lifts rates when inflation is the worry. Growth data lifts rates and leaves stocks mixed. Check the regime first, because good news can be bad news when the Fed is worried about inflation.

Dates can slip. A government shutdown can delay or cancel a release: the BLS tables carry the note "Data unavailable due to the 2025 lapse in appropriations" for October 2025 CPI.

| Meeting | Statement day, 2:00 p.m. ET | Summary of Economic Projections and dot plot |
|---|---|---|
| January 27 to 28 | January 28 | No |
| March 17 to 18 | March 18 | Yes |
| April 28 to 29 | April 29 | No |
| June 16 to 17 | June 17 | Yes |
| July 28 to 29 | July 29 | No |
| September 15 to 16 | September 16 | Yes |

The projections come with the March, June, September and December meetings only. Minutes follow three weeks after each meeting. See [The Fed and the FOMC](../11-macro-and-economic-data/02-the-fed-and-the-fomc.md).

## Using the calendar

Rank each release as high, medium or low, and write your rule for each high item before the day. A simple ranking: high for CPI, the jobs report and FOMC days, medium for GDP, PCE, retail sales and the ISM PMIs, low for the rest. An 8:30 a.m. release lands 60 minutes before the 9:30 a.m. open, and the 2:00 p.m. FOMC statement lands 120 minutes before the 4:00 p.m. close.
